LINER NOTES

Have you felt it this year, even for a split second? The fear that a text file or a line of code could do your job better than you can. Execution got cheap. THE RECEIPT

What your people walk out with

  1. 01

    You’re the loop, not one more link in it

    Systems have always drawn us a box and pushed us in. Now there’s technology that does what’s inside the box far faster than we do. The talk flips the equation: from waiting to be replaced, to seeing that the quirk, the scatter or the scar each person carries is exactly what multiplies them in this new world.

  2. 02

    Who are you once the labels come off?

    What happens the day the label is taken away? AI isn’t only at the office anymore. It meets us everywhere, and when an algorithm writes, plans and calculates better than we do, that’s unsettling. It’s also where the real value shows up. The room rebuilds a backbone on one simple idea: I connect my own dots. An “I” that doesn’t hang on a company, a team or a status that could change tomorrow morning.

  3. 03

    Tomorrow, 9:00, at the screen (no bullshit)

    Keeping your head down until the AI wave passes isn’t an option, and philosophy in the air isn’t enough. Everyone leaves with one concrete move, usable and a little pushy, that stops them waiting for someone else to build it for them or give them permission. How to put this technology to work in their own day, in a way that brings them back to themselves.

And why me?

Because I walked this road myself. I didn’t read about it, I lived it. From losing the title and peeling off the labels, to building products, a team of agents and Cleo, all without writing a line of code. I’m not coming to talk to your people about inspiration. I’m coming to show them the exact road I took.

WHY ANAT · FROM THE INSIDE

Why Anat?

For as long as I can remember, when people ask me who I am, I answer: I connect the dots.

Life asked a lot of me. A third culture kid who grew up in Kenya, finding my way between systems of rules and borders. A first chapter, a second chapter, two families joined into one. Employed, self-employed, employed again. Diagnosed with ADHD at 36. A manager with a track record who was let go twice, always feeling I had far more to give than the organization allowed.

Read the rest →

Today I see that it was all connecting dots. The system just didn’t know how to file it, so it called it “scattered”, drew me a box and pushed me in.

In January 2026 they took my label away. So I did the one thing I knew how to do: I went and built what would multiply me. A product, a team of agents, and Cleo, who answers her own email. All without a single line of code in my background. For the record.

When that happens, when it’s allowed, the ability every one of us has shows up again. The twist, the quirk, the scar. Call it what you like. It’s the part that’s worth something, and it’s you.

Along the way I meet plenty of people who say “I’m comfortable, work is like family”, and organizations that are only experimenting, carefully. But this isn’t going anywhere. There’s technology here that can make us bigger and bring us back to ourselves, if we learn to use it well. Ignore it, keep our heads down and wait for it to pass? That’s just not an option.

Let’s talk. It’ll be interesting.
